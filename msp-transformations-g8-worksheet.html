1️⃣ Translations (Slides)

Every point moves the same distance in the same direction. (x, y) → (x + a, y + b).

Practice:

  1. Translate (3, 5) by (2, -1). New point?
  2. Translate (-2, 4) by (5, 3). New?
  3. Translate (0, 0) by (-4, -6). New?

Answer Key — Section 1

(1) (5, 4) (2) (3, 7) (3) (-4, -6)

2️⃣ Reflections (Flips)

Over the x-axis: (x, y) → (x, -y). Over the y-axis: (x, y) → (-x, y).

Practice:

  1. Reflect (3, 4) over x-axis. New?
  2. Reflect (3, 4) over y-axis. New?
  3. Reflect (-2, -5) over x-axis. New?
  4. Reflect (-2, -5) over y-axis. New?
—

Answer Key — Section 2

(1) (3, -4) (2) (-3, 4) (3) (-2, 5) (4) (2, -5)

3️⃣ Rotations (Turns)

Around origin: 90° clockwise: (x,y) → (y, -x). 180°: (x, y) → (-x, -y). 90° counter-clockwise: (x,y) → (-y, x).

Practice:

  1. Rotate (3, 4) 180° around origin. New?
  2. Rotate (3, 4) 90° CW. New?
  3. Rotate (3, 4) 90° CCW. New?
—

Answer Key — Section 3

(1) (-3, -4) (2) (4, -3) (3) (-4, 3)

4️⃣ Dilations (Resize)

Multiply both coordinates by scale factor k. (x, y) → (kx, ky). k > 1 enlarges; 0 < k < 1 shrinks.

Practice:

  1. Dilate (3, 4) by k = 2. New?
  2. Dilate (6, 8) by k = ½. New?
  3. Dilate (-2, 5) by k = 3. New?
—

Answer Key — Section 4

(1) (6, 8) (2) (3, 4) (3) (-6, 15)

5️⃣ Which Preserves Size/Shape?

Translations, reflections, rotations = RIGID motions (same size). Dilations change SIZE but keep shape.

Practice:

  1. Which changes size?
  2. Which preserves distance between points?
  3. Which is a "flip"?
  4. Which is a "turn"?
—

Answer Key — Section 5

(1) Dilation (2) Translation, reflection, rotation (3) Reflection (4) Rotation

6️⃣ Prove Congruence with a Sequence of Transformations

Two figures are CONGRUENT if one can be mapped onto the other by a sequence of RIGID motions (translations, reflections, rotations — in any combination). Rigid motions preserve BOTH size and shape.
CCSS 8.G.2 says: Understand that a two-dimensional figure is congruent to another if the second can be obtained from the first by a sequence of rotations, reflections, and translations.

You must be able to DESCRIBE the sequence, not just perform each step.

How to describe a sequence:

  1. Identify a matching point on both figures.
  2. Ask: what single motion moves that first point to the matching second point?
  3. Check the other matching points; if they don't yet line up, add another transformation.
  4. State each transformation clearly (e.g., "Translate 3 right and 2 up, then reflect over the y-axis").

Practice — Describe a sequence of rigid motions to prove congruence:

  1. Triangle A has vertices (0,0), (2,0), (0,3). Triangle B has vertices (5,0), (7,0), (5,3). What single transformation maps A onto B?
  2. Triangle A: (0,0), (2,0), (0,3). Triangle B: (0,0), (-2,0), (0,3). Describe the transformation.
  3. Triangle A: (1,1), (3,1), (3,4). Triangle B: (1,-1), (3,-1), (3,-4). Describe.
  4. Triangle A: (0,0), (4,0), (0,3). Triangle B: (5,2), (9,2), (5,5). Describe the sequence.
  5. Square A: (0,0), (2,0), (2,2), (0,2). Square B: (3,-1), (5,-1), (5,1), (3,1). Describe.
  6. Triangle A: (0,0), (3,0), (0,4). Triangle B: (0,0), (0,3), (-4,0). Describe (hint: rotation).

Show that these ARE congruent (yes/no + reason):

  1. Both triangles have sides 3-4-5. Congruent?
  2. One triangle has sides 3, 4, 5. Another has sides 6, 8, 10. Congruent?
  3. Two rectangles both 4×6. Congruent?
  4. Two rectangles: 4×6 and 3×8 (same area). Congruent?
Key idea: Rigid motions (translations, reflections, rotations) prove CONGRUENCE. Dilations don't — dilations change size, so they prove SIMILARITY (same shape, different size).

Answer Key — Section 6

Sequences:

(1) Translate 5 right (each vertex shifts by (5, 0)).

(2) Reflect over the y-axis (or x-axis flip won't work — check: (2,0) → (-2,0) ✓).

(3) Reflect over the x-axis.

(4) Translate 5 right AND 2 up. Check all three vertices.

(5) Translate 3 right AND 1 down.

(6) Rotate 90° counter-clockwise around origin. (3,0) → (0,3) ✓ and (0,4) → (-4,0) ✓.

Congruent yes/no:

(1) Yes — SSS congruence (same three sides), so a rigid motion sequence exists.

(2) NO — same shape but bigger. That's SIMILAR (dilation ×2), not congruent.

(3) Yes — same dimensions, so a rigid motion sequence maps one to the other.

(4) NO — same AREA but different dimensions; no rigid motion turns 4×6 into 3×8.

📐 Formulas (always available for reference):
Error = |Measured − Actual|
Percent Error = |Measured − Actual| ÷ Actual × 100
Percent error < absolute error can mislead — a big number of grams may still be a very small % of the real value.
  1. Convert 4.5 km to meters.
  2. Convert 2,500 mL to liters.
  3. Convert 750 g to kilograms.
  4. A student measures a cat as 7 kg. Actual mass is 8 kg. Compute the ERROR (in kg) and the PERCENT ERROR.
  5. A student measures a blue whale as 175,000 kg. Actual mass is 180,000 kg. Compute the ERROR (in kg) and the PERCENT ERROR.
  6. The whale measurement has a MUCH larger error in kilograms (5,000 kg vs 1 kg). Which measurement is actually MORE ACCURATE, and why? Explain using percent error.
  7. You weigh a piece of paper as 0.9 g (actual 1.0 g) vs an elephant as 5,900 kg (actual 6,000 kg). Compute percent error for both. Which is more accurate?
  8. Which graph best shows PARTS OF A WHOLE (percentages of beach trash)?
  9. Which graph best shows the RELATIONSHIP between two continuous variables (ocean depth vs temperature)?
  10. Find the mean (average) and range of: 4, 6, 8, 10, 12

Answer Key

(1) 4,500 m | (2) 2.5 L | (3) 0.75 kg | (4) Error = 1 kg; %Error = 1/8 × 100 = 12.5% | (5) Error = 5,000 kg; %Error = 5000/180000 × 100 = 2.78% | (6) The whale (2.78% error is much smaller than 12.5%). Absolute error can mislead — 5,000 kg sounds huge, but relative to a 180,000 kg whale it's less than 3%. Percent error compares the mistake to the size of the thing being measured, so it's the fair way to compare accuracy across very different scales. | (7) Paper: |0.9−1.0|/1.0 × 100 = 10%. Elephant: |5900−6000|/6000 × 100 = 1.67%. The elephant measurement is more accurate — 100 kg off a 6,000 kg animal is a smaller percent of the real value than 0.1 g off a 1 g paper. | (8) Pie chart (circle graph) | (9) Scatter plot (X-Y plot) | (10) Mean = (4+6+8+10+12)/5 = 40/5 = 8; Range = 12 − 4 = 8

🚢 Foil Boat & Skittles

Mary, Star of the Sea School · 6th Grade Math · Density Lab

The big idea: Design a foil boat. First test it with pennies (2.5 g each) to check that your design floats. Then load Skittles (1 g each) as passengers — the challenge is to load as many as possible without sinking. 🍬 If you reach 90% of your predicted capacity, you get to eat the Skittles!

🧰 What You Need

  • 1 square of aluminum foil, 7.5 cm × 7.5 cm
  • Cup of pennies (about 20, 2.5 g each) — for the design test
  • Cup of Skittles (about 60, 1 g each) — for the passenger challenge
  • Bowl or tub of water
  • Ruler in cm
  • Paper towels
  • Extra cup for leftover Skittles (🍬 snack time if you win the challenge!)

🔬 What to Do

Shape a boat. Fold the foil into a rectangle shape with walls about 1.25 cm tall. No holes!
Measure the inside in cm: length, width, and height of the walls.
Calculate the volume: Volume = length × width × height.
Predict: Predict the max grams (= Volume), max pennies (= V ÷ 2.5), and max Skittles (= V).
Test with pennies. Load pennies one at a time. Check that your boat design works.
Empty and dry, then load Skittles. Add Skittles as passengers — count until it sinks.
Snack check! If you loaded ≥ 90% of your prediction, you earn the Skittles. 🍬

Max grams the boat can hold = Boat volume (cm³)
→ Max pennies = V ÷ 2.5  ·  Max Skittles = V
Water weighs 1 gram for every 1 cm³. So a boat with 30 cm³ of space can hold 30 grams — that's 30 ÷ 2.5 = 12 pennies, OR 30 Skittles (since each Skittle ≈ 1 g). Skittles let you fit more "passengers" because they're lighter!

⚖️ Check with D = m ÷ V

At the moment your boat sank, its density should match the density of WATER (1.0 g/cm³). Let's check!

The density formula
D = m ÷ V
m = total mass at sink (Skittles × 1 g)  ·  V = boat volume (cm³)  ·  D = density (g/cm³)

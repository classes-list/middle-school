🚢 Foil Boat & Skittles

Mary, Star of the Sea School · 6th Grade Math · Density Lab

The big idea: Make a boat out of aluminum foil. Add Skittles one by one as passengers. Guess how many Skittles it will hold before you start. Then test it! (One Skittle weighs about 1 gram — that's what makes this fun.)

🧰 What You Need

  • 1 square of aluminum foil, 12 cm × 12 cm
  • Cup of Skittles (about 150 — grab a handful more than you think you'll need)
  • Bowl or tub of water
  • Ruler in cm
  • Paper towels
  • Extra cup for leftover Skittles (🍬 snack time after!)

🔬 What to Do

Shape a boat. Fold the foil into a rectangle shape with walls about 2 cm tall. No holes!
Measure the inside in cm: length, width, and height of the walls.
Calculate the volume: Volume = length × width × height.
Predict: The volume in cm³ IS your Skittle guess! (Because 1 Skittle ≈ 1 g and 1 cm³ of water lifts 1 g.)
Test it! Float the boat. Add Skittles one at a time. Count until it sinks.
Compare your guess with what really happened.

Your boat is 8 cm long, 8 cm wide, and 2 cm tall walls.

Step 1 — Volume:  V = 8 × 8 × 2 = 128 cm³

Step 2 — Max weight:  128 cm³ × 1 g/cm³ = 128 g  (because water weighs 1 g per cm³)

Step 3 — Skittles:  128 g ÷ 1 g/Skittle = 128 Skittles  (1 gram per Skittle makes this easy!)

In real life, it might sink at fewer Skittles because water splashes in before the walls are fully underwater. That's normal!

⚖️ Check with D = m ÷ V

At the moment your boat sank, its density should match the density of WATER (1.0 g/cm³). Let's check!

The density formula
D = m ÷ V
m = total mass at sink (Skittles × 1 g)  ·  V = boat volume (cm³)  ·  D = density (g/cm³)

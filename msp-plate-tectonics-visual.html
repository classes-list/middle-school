🌋 Plate Tectonics & Rates

A few centimeters a year, over millions of years, moves whole continents.

Big idea: Tectonic plates move at rates in centimeters per year. That's absurdly slow — your fingernail grows about as fast. But over millions of years, the ratio "distance ÷ time" adds up to continents. This is why rates matter: small numbers × huge time = enormous distance.

💡 Real Hawaii context: The Pacific Plate has been sliding northwest over a stationary hot spot at about 7 cm/year for millions of years. That's why the Hawaiian Islands form a chain — each island was made over the hot spot, then dragged away. Kauai is about 5 million years old, so it's roughly 350 km NW of Big Island. 🧭 What Makes This a Rate Problem

Every rate has the form quantity per one time unit. Plate motion is centimeters per year — same structure as miles per hour, dollars per pound, or heartbeats per minute. Once you know the rate, distance is just multiplication:

distance = rate × time

The trick is unit conversion. Rates come in cm/year. Big time spans come in millions of years. Distances come out in kilometers. Watch how many decimal places move as centimeters climb into kilometers — that's the KHDUdcm staircase in action.

📖 Background

Data tables and graphing are used by scientists to display the data that is collected during a controlled experiment. A graph must be constructed to accurately depict the data collected. An incorrect graph often leads to the acceptance of an incorrect hypothesis or detract from the acceptance of a correct hypothesis.

The graph should contain 4 major parts: the title, the independent variable, the dependent variable, and the units for each variable.

  • The Title: shows what the graph is about. Reading the title should give the reader an idea of the variables displayed. Concise, placed above the graph, and stated as a relationship or comparison.
  • Independent Variable: the variable the experimenter changes / manipulates. Placed on the horizontal (x) axis.
  • Dependent Variable: the variable directly affected by the independent variable — the result of what happens. Placed on the vertical (y) axis.
  • Units: in parentheses next to your independent and dependent variables.

❓ How do I know what type of graph to use?

→ X-Y Scatter Plot

The most common type of graph. Used to determine the relationship between two variables. If both increase together they have a positive relationship; if one decreases as the other increases, the relationship is negative. Relationships can be linear or exponential; sometimes there is no relationship at all. If the graph is proportional, directly proportional, or inversely proportional, identify it as such.

→ Column Chart / Bar Graph

Used to compare two or more different groups. Bar graphs do not show a relationship and do not change over time.

→ Pie Chart / Circle Graph

Best when you are comparing parts of a whole. Pie charts do not show a relationship and do not change over time.

🏋️ Gym Membership Showdown

Two gyms, two prices — use slope and y-intercept to decide which is the smarter buy.

🎯 The Situation

You want to join a gym for the year. You find two options with different prices. Both charge a one-time sign-up fee, plus a monthly rate.

Question: Which gym is cheaper depends on how many months you plan to stay. Your job: figure out the answer using y = mx + b.
💪 Gym A — "FitLife"
  • Sign-up fee: $30 (paid once)
  • Monthly rate: $20/month
Equation: try to figure it out ↓
🏋️ Gym B — "PeakForm"
  • Sign-up fee: $50 (paid once)
  • Monthly rate: $15/month
Equation: try to figure it out ↓

1️⃣ Set Up the Equations

Write each cost as an equation in slope-intercept form: y = mx + b, where y = total cost and x = number of months.

💪 Gym A — FitLife

$ per month
$ paid once
The sign-up fee is b (the y-intercept) because you pay it at x = 0 months. It's your starting cost before any monthly charges.

The monthly rate is m (the slope) because it's how much your total goes UP for each additional month.

🏋️ Gym B — PeakForm

$ per month
$ paid once

Each line shows total cost after x months. Where they cross is the break-even point.

4️⃣ Make a Recommendation

Based on your break-even point, which gym is better for each of these people? Pick from the dropdowns.

Alex plans to join for just 2 months for summer training.

Riley is committing for the whole year — 12 months.

Sam is planning exactly 4 months.

Break-even at x = 4 splits the decision:
  • Fewer than 4 months → Gym A wins (lower sign-up fee matters more than the monthly rate).
  • Exactly 4 months → Tie — same total cost, both $110.
  • More than 4 months → Gym B wins (lower monthly rate saves you $5/month, which adds up).

🎓 What You Learned

Every real-life "sign-up fee + monthly rate" deal is a linear equation. You now know how to:

  • Turn a real price offer into y = mx + b (sign-up fee = b, monthly rate = m)
  • Graph two options and find the break-even point where they cross
  • Solve the equations algebraically to confirm the graph
  • Use the break-even to recommend the cheaper option based on time

These same skills apply to: cell phone plans, streaming subscriptions, ride-share vs. bike, storage rentals, and dozens of everyday decisions.

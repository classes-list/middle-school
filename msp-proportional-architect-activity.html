📐 Architect's Blueprint — Scale-Down a Real Building

Mary, Star of the Sea School · 7th Grade Math · Dr. Park

Name: Date: Class:
The big idea: A scale drawing is a proportional relationship in action. Every real measurement gets multiplied by the same number k — the scale factor — to get the drawing measurement. Same rule as always: y = k · x. This time k is a fraction because we're shrinking a building down to fit on paper.

Your Mission

Pick a real building. Look up (or measure) its real dimensions. Choose a scale factor that lets it fit on 8½" × 11" paper. Draw the floor plan (top-down view) to scale. Then answer the reflection questions.

Choose your building

  1. Mary, Star of the Sea School (recommended — walk and measure it yourself)
  2. Your own house or apartment (measure with a family member)
  3. A famous building (White House 168' × 152', Alamo, Fallingwater, Independence Hall, Taj Mahal base 240' × 240')
  4. A dream house from Zillow (listings show sq ft; some include floor plans)

✨ Worked Example — Mary, Star of the Sea School

Given (example dimensions — measure your own!)

Suppose the school's main building is:

  • Length = 160 ft
  • Width = 80 ft
  • Height = 30 ft

Choose a scale

Try the "1 ft = ⅒ inch" scale. That means:

1 ft = 0.1 in   ⇒   scale factor k = 0.1 in ÷ 12 in = 1/120

Written as a ratio: 1 : 120  (every 120 real inches becomes 1 drawing inch).

Apply k to every dimension

DimensionReal× k (=1/120)Scaled
Length160 ft160 × 0.1 in/ft16 in
Width80 ft80 × 0.1 in/ft8 in
Height30 ft30 × 0.1 in/ft3 in

Check: Does 16 in × 8 in fit on 11 in × 8½ in paper?   No — 16" is too long. Try a smaller scale.

Try a smaller scale — 1 ft = 1/16 inch

1 ft = 0.0625 in  ⇒  k = 1/192   (ratio 1 : 192)

DimensionReal× kScaled
Length160 ft160 × 0.062510 in
Width80 ft80 × 0.06255 in
Height30 ft30 × 0.06251.875 in

10 in × 5 in fits on 11 × 8½ paper with room for labels. ✓ Use this scale.

The Big Surprise: linear scales by k, but AREA scales by k² and VOLUME by k³.

The school's real floor area is 160 × 80 = 12,800 sq ft. Your drawing's floor area is 10 × 5 = 50 sq in. Convert: 12,800 sq ft × 144 sq in/sq ft = 1,843,200 sq in. Ratio: 1,843,200 ÷ 50 = 36,864. And 192² = 36,864. ✓ Area really does scale by k².

📝 Draw Your Floor Plan

Use the scale you chose above. Measure once, draw twice — check every wall length against your scaled dimensions before you commit to a line.

Common mistake: Drawing the outer walls first, then squeezing rooms in. Instead, plan the layout on scratch paper: how many rooms, what shape, where are the doors? Then draw to scale.

🤔 Reflection Questions

1. Write your scale as a ratio: 1 : ______. Show your work — how did you get this from "1 ft = ___ inches"?
2. Real length of your building = ______ ft. Scaled length = ______ in. Show that scaled ÷ real (with matching units!) equals your k.
3. Real floor area = ______ sq ft. Scaled floor area = ______ sq in. Show the ratio and check it equals k².
4. Real volume = ______ cubic ft. Scaled volume = ______ cubic in. Compare the ratio to k³. What is the shocking result?
5. If your scaled building doubled in size on paper (2× every side) but the real building stayed the same, what happens to k? What happens to the area ratio? Explain in one sentence.
6. Real-world payoff: Give one job or situation (besides architects) where people use scale drawings every day. Explain how proportional reasoning is essential in that job.

1️⃣ Place Value Review

Every digit's value depends on its place. To the LEFT of the decimal: ones, tens, hundreds, thousands… To the RIGHT: tenths, hundredths, thousandths, ten-thousandths.
Reading decimals: Read the whole part, say "and" for the decimal point, then read the fraction part with its place-value name. Example: 3.47 = "three and forty-seven hundredths."

Practice:

  1. Write in words: 5.3
  2. Write in words: 0.06
  3. Write in words: 12.409
  4. Which is larger: 0.7 or 0.65?
  5. Which is larger: 3.14 or 3.104?
  6. Round 4.867 to the nearest tenth.
  7. Round 12.352 to the nearest hundredth.

2️⃣ Adding & Subtracting Decimals

Line up the decimal points! Add zeros to make the numbers the same length. Then add or subtract as usual — bring the decimal straight down.
Golden rule: DECIMALS LINE UP. Missing places → add zeros. Example: 3.4 − 1.276 → write 3.400 − 1.276 = 2.124.

3️⃣ Multiplying Decimals

Multiply as if there were no decimal points. Then count the total number of decimal places in both factors — put that many places in the answer.
Steps: (1) Ignore decimals, multiply. (2) Count decimal places in BOTH factors. (3) Add them, and move the decimal that many places from the right of the answer.

4️⃣ Dividing Decimals

When the divisor (outside) has a decimal, move BOTH decimals the same number of places right until the divisor is a whole number. Then divide normally, bringing the decimal straight up.
Trick: Move decimals to make the divisor a whole number. Move BOTH the same number of places. Example: 4.5 ÷ 0.3 → 45 ÷ 3 = 15.

5️⃣ Word Problems

Real-world decimals: money, measurements, distances, science lab data. Identify the operation, then apply the rules above.

Practice:

  1. A pizza slice costs $2.75. What is the total cost for 4 slices?
  2. You bought a shirt for $18.99 and paid with a $20 bill. How much change?
  3. A car goes 32.5 miles on 1.25 gallons of gas. What is the miles per gallon (mpg)?
  4. A lab beaker holds 0.85 L. How many mL is that? (1 L = 1000 mL)
  5. A recipe needs 0.5 kg of flour for 4 servings. How much for 10 servings?

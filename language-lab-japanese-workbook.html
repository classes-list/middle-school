🌏 Culture Corner

  • Japanese uses three writing systems: hiragana (native words), katakana (loan words), and kanji (Chinese characters).
  • In Hawai'i, over 200,000 residents claim Japanese ancestry -- words like bento, sushi, and shibai are part of local life.
  • Japanese has no plural forms and no articles (a / the) -- meaning comes from context.
  • Bowing (ojigi) is a common greeting; the deeper the bow, the more respect it shows.
  • The verb goes at the END of a Japanese sentence: 私は寿司を食べます (I sushi eat).

📐 Mini-Grammar: word order & 'desu'

Japanese word order is Subject + Object + Verb.
The word です (desu) is like the English 'is / am / are' and goes at the end of the sentence: 私は学生です (Watashi wa gakusei desu.) = I am a student.

  2. Count from 1 to 20 aloud in Japanese.
  3. Say today's day, month, and weather.
  4. Draw your family and label each person in Japanese.

📐 Mini-Grammar: particles を (o) and に (ni)

Particles are little words that tag other words in a Japanese sentence.
を (o) marks the direct object: 寿司を食べます (I eat sushi).
に (ni) marks a destination or time: 学校に行きます (I go to school). 三時に寝ます (I sleep at 3:00).

  2. Order your favorite Japanese food using ...を食べます / ...を飲みます.
  3. Describe your best friend using three adjectives.
  4. Say what time you wake up and go to sleep.

📐 Mini-Grammar: past tense -mashita

Polite past tense: swap -masu for -mashita. 食べます (tabemasu = eat) → 食べました (tabemashita = ate). 行きます (ikimasu = go) → 行きました (ikimashita = went).

  3. Plan a day in Tokyo: three places + why.
  4. Ask for prices at three imaginary shops.

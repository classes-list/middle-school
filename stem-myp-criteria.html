MYP STEM Criteria A, B, C, D

The four dimensions of scientific work we score — and how the 0–8 scores turn into your child’s percentage grade.

📊 What MYP Criteria Measure

The International Baccalaureate Middle Years Programme (MYP) uses four criteria for science / STEM. Each is scored 0–8 against detailed rubric descriptors. Together they answer one question: which dimension of scientific work is being demonstrated?

Unlike a percentage grade, each criterion score is a description of what a student can currently do, not a ranking. A student can be at level 8 on Criterion A and level 4 on Criterion D at the same time — and both matter.

🎯 The Four Criteria

A

Knowing & Understanding

Recall and apply scientific knowledge. Explain phenomena and use scientific vocabulary correctly.

Score 0–8
B

Inquiring & Designing

First half of the experimental / engineering design cycle: ask a testable question, form a hypothesis, plan a fair experiment, design a solution.

Score 0–8
C

Processing & Evaluating

Second half of the cycle: collect and record data, analyze results, draw conclusions supported by evidence, evaluate the method.

Score 0–8
D

Reflecting on the Impacts of Science / Engineering

Consider the ethical, environmental, cultural, and social implications of scientific and engineering decisions.

Score 0–8

📝 How Rubric Levels Are Described

Each 0–8 score has descriptor language teachers use to score consistently. Broadly:

  • Levels 1–2: Attempts limited work; needs teacher prompting for basic steps.
  • Levels 3–4: Applies procedures in familiar situations; some accurate work with support.
  • Levels 5–6: Selects and applies procedures in familiar and less familiar situations; explains reasoning.
  • Levels 7–8: Selects and applies procedures in unfamiliar situations independently; justifies reasoning and evaluates results.

The top strand (7–8) requires higher-order thinking — students must analyze data, evaluate methods, or design new investigations, not just execute a procedure.

❓ Common Questions

Q: Which criterion is the most important?
None — A, B, C, and D are horizontal categories, not a hierarchy. A well-rounded scientist / engineer needs all four.

Q: Do all four criteria show up on every assignment?
No. A quiz might assess only Criterion A. A project might assess B, C, and D but not A. Each task is scored on whichever criteria fit.

Q: Can my child raise a Criterion D score later in the year?
Yes. Because MYP scores describe current ability, later evidence of growth on the same criterion supersedes earlier scores.

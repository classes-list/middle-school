1️⃣ Ratio Notation

A ratio compares two quantities. Three equivalent notations: 3 to 5, 3:5, 3/5.

Practice:

  1. In a class of 24 students, 10 are boys. Boys : girls =
  2. A recipe uses 2 cups flour to 3 cups milk. Flour : milk =
  3. A team has 15 wins and 5 losses. Wins : losses (simplified) =
  4. In a bag: 8 red, 12 blue marbles. Red : total =
  5. Class 6:1 has 18 boys and 12 girls. Boys : girls (simplified) =

Answer Key — Section 1

(1) 10 : 14 = 5 : 7 · (2) 2 : 3 · (3) 15 : 5 = 3 : 1 · (4) 8 : 20 = 2 : 5 · (5) 18 : 12 = 3 : 2

2️⃣ Equivalent Ratios & Simplifying

Two ratios are equivalent if their fractions are equal. To simplify, divide both parts by the GCF.

Practice:

  1. Simplify: 15 : 25
  2. Simplify: 24 : 36
  3. Simplify: 8 : 20
  4. Find missing: 3 : 4 = 15 : ?
  5. Find missing: 2 : 5 = ? : 20

Answer Key — Section 2

(1) 3 : 5 · (2) 2 : 3 · (3) 2 : 5 · (4) 20 · (5) 8

3️⃣ Unit Rates

A UNIT RATE has 1 in the denominator. Divide numerator ÷ denominator to find it.
Units help you check: MILES per HOUR, DOLLARS per POUND, WORDS per MINUTE, etc.

Practice:

  1. 180 miles in 3 hours → mph =
  2. $24 for 6 lbs → cost per lb =
  3. 320 words typed in 4 minutes → wpm =
  4. 72 heartbeats in 60 seconds → beats per second =
  5. $3.75 for 3 apples → cost per apple =

Answer Key — Section 3

(1) 60 mph · (2) $4/lb · (3) 80 wpm · (4) 1.2 beats/s · (5) $1.25/apple

4️⃣ Complex Fractions in Unit Rates

Sometimes both parts of a ratio are fractions. Apply the Keep-Change-Flip rule to divide.
Example: Ali walks 1/2 mile in 1/4 hour. Rate = (1/2) ÷ (1/4) = 1/2 × 4/1 = 2 miles per hour.

Practice:

  1. Walk 3/4 mile in 1/2 hour. Rate?
  2. Read 2/3 of a book in 1/4 week. Books per week?
  3. Paint 1/3 wall in 1/6 hour. Walls per hour?
  4. Fill 5/8 tank in 1/2 hour. Tanks per hour?

Answer Key — Section 4

(1) 3/4 ÷ 1/2 = 1.5 mph · (2) 2/3 ÷ 1/4 = 8/3 books/week ≈ 2.67 · (3) 1/3 ÷ 1/6 = 2 walls/hr · (4) 5/8 ÷ 1/2 = 5/4 tanks/hr = 1.25

5️⃣ Best Buy Comparisons

Which is the better deal? Convert each option to a unit rate ($/unit), then compare — smaller unit price = better deal.

Practice:

  1. Option A: 12 oz for $3.60. Option B: 20 oz for $5.20. Better?
  2. Option A: 3 lbs of apples for $6. Option B: 5 lbs for $9. Better?
  3. Small box: 8 crackers for $2. Large box: 20 crackers for $4.50. Better?

Answer Key — Section 5

(1) A: $0.30/oz, B: $0.26/oz → B is better · (2) A: $2/lb, B: $1.80/lb → B is better · (3) Small: $0.25/cracker, Large: $0.225/cracker → Large is better

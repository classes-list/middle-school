📉 Richter Scale & Powers of 10

Compare two earthquakes. Watch the shaking multiply by 10, 100, or 1,000×.

Big idea: Each step up the Richter scale multiplies ground motion by 10. A magnitude 6 shakes 10× harder than a magnitude 5. A magnitude 7 shakes 100× harder. A magnitude 8 shakes 1,000×. This is a logarithmic scale — small differences in the number mean enormous differences in reality.

Pick Two Earthquakes

Earthquake A

M 4.0
Felt indoors, no damage

Earthquake B

M 7.0
Major, serious damage
B shakes how many times harder than A?
1,000× stronger ground motion
10^(7 − 4) = 10^3 = 1,000
B releases how much more ENERGY than A?
31,623× more energy
Each magnitude step ≈ 32× more energy → 32^3 ≈ 31,623×
💡 Two ratios, not one: ground motion multiplies by 10 per step, but the energy released multiplies by roughly 32 per step. That's why a M9 is not just "a little worse than a M8" — it releases about 32× more energy.

🌏 The Richter Ladder

M 1–2
Micro — barely detectable, only instruments notice
M 3
Minor — light rattling of dishes
M 4
Light — felt indoors, ceiling fans sway
M 5
Moderate — cracked plaster, small objects fall (typical for Hawaii tremors)
M 6
Strong — significant damage in populated areas
M 7
Major — serious damage over large areas (2018 Kīlauea M 6.9)
M 8
Great — catastrophic destruction (1946 Aleutian tsunami reached Hawaii)
M 9+
Devastating — Sumatra 2004 (M 9.1), Japan 2011 (M 9.1)

🧮 The Math Underneath

The Richter scale uses powers of 10. If Earthquake A is magnitude mA and Earthquake B is magnitude mB, then:

amplitude ratio = 10^(mB − mA)

  • 1 magnitude apart → 10× more ground motion
  • 2 magnitudes apart → 10² = 100× more
  • 3 magnitudes apart → 10³ = 1,000× more

Powers of 10 are exactly the same math as the KHDUdcm staircase in metric conversions — each step means multiply by 10. Once you get the pattern, both feel natural.

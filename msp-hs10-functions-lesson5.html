📘 How It Works

Key idea: y = a·f(x − h) + k has vertex (h, k).

For y = a·f(x − h) + k, the parent’s key point (vertex) moves to (h, k). Read h with the opposite sign of what is written inside; read k as written. The value a only stretches or flips — it does not move the vertex.

👀 Watch Two Examples

✏️ Worked Example 1
The graph of y = -2|x + 4| + 1 is a transformed |x|. What is the x-coordinate of its vertex?
Answer: -4
✏️ Worked Example 2
The graph of y = (x + 5)² − 4 is a transformed x². What is the x-coordinate of its vertex?
Answer: -5

🛒 Best Buy Calculator — Kalihi Edition

Auntie Malia's Sunday potluck. Three stores. One ratio question.

🎯 The Situation

Auntie Malia is hosting her family's Sunday potluck in Kalihi. She has $80, a car, and three stores on the way home: Times Supermarket Kalihi, Don Quijote on Nimitz, and Costco Iwilei. Every store sells the same items — but in different sizes at different prices. Which package is actually the best buy?

That's a ratios and rates problem in disguise. This page is a live calculator you can play with — change any price, size, or store and watch the answer update.

🧠 The Ratios Behind the Answer

Ratio #1 — Unit Price is a Rate

You cannot compare a 12 oz can of Spam ($3.99) to a 96 oz Costco pack ($22.99) by looking at the price alone — the sizes are wildly different. To compare fairly, you need to reduce every option to the same "per one" scale. That's a unit rate:

Unit price = price ÷ size   →   dollars per one ounce (or per one pound)

For a 12 oz can at $3.99: 3.99 ÷ 12 = $0.3325 per oz. Every problem in this unit — miles per hour, students per teacher, cost per pound — is the same move: quantity ÷ how many. That is what makes it a rate.

Ratio #2 — Best Buy = Smallest Unit Price (Within an Item)

Once every option has a unit price, the winner is just the smallest number… but only within the same item. You cannot say rice is a better buy than Spam because rice is $/lb and Spam is $/oz — they aren't the same rate. The calculator compares Spam only to Spam, rice only to rice, and shoyu only to shoyu.

Ratio #3 — How Much Worse? Percent More Expensive

Column "% more expensive than best" asks a ratio question: how much bigger is this option's unit price than the best one's, relative to the best one?

% more expensive = (this unit price − best unit price) ÷ best unit price

For the 12 oz Spam vs the 96 oz Costco pack: (0.3325 − 0.2395) ÷ 0.2395 = 0.3884 = 38.84%. So the small can costs almost 40% more per ounce than the big pack. That is a ratio comparison — you're measuring the gap as a fraction of the winner, not just the raw difference.

💡 When Does Shopping at Costco Actually Make Sense?

Run the numbers with the original prices and Costco wins every single item. So Auntie Malia should just shop entirely at Costco… right?

Most Kalihi families don't. Here are the times when the cheapest unit price is the best real-life deal — and the times when it's not:

Costco makes sense when…

  • 🍚 The item doesn't go bad quickly. 25 lb of Diamond G rice in a rice canister keeps for months. 40 oz of shoyu in the pantry lasts a year. These are the items where the big pack pays off.
  • 👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 The household is big enough to consume it. A family of six eating rice at every dinner blows through 25 lb quickly. A single retiree does not.
  • 🛒 You're making enough Costco trips per year to amortize the $65 membership. If you shop there once a month, the membership costs ~$5.42 per trip. If you shop there twice a year, it costs $32.50 per trip.
  • 🚗 You're combining errands, so the gas cost of the extra drive is already sunk (you were driving to Iwilei anyway).

Costco does NOT make sense when…

  • 🥫 The item expires or goes stale before you can finish it. 96 oz of Spam is 8 cans. If your family eats one can every 2 weeks, the last cans sit for four months — and once opened, refrigerated Spam only lasts about a week.
  • 💵 The upfront cost busts your budget. Auntie Malia's $34.99 rice at Costco is cheaper per pound, but it eats over 40% of her $80 budget in a single item.
  • ⏱ You rarely go to Iwilei. If the round trip costs $7 in gas plus 45 minutes, and you saved $8 on groceries, the "deal" is basically break-even.
  • 🏠 You have no place to store the big pack. A 25 lb rice bag needs a canister; a 96 oz Spam pack needs freezer or fridge shelf space.
The pattern: unit price is the right way to compare across sizes. But real decisions also weigh storage, freshness, upfront cash, and travel cost. A rate is a powerful tool — not the whole story.

🤔 Real-Life Questions

Answer each of these in a Google Doc or on paper. These are the times when the best unit price is not the best deal:
  1. Auntie Malia's family is 6 people. Costco Spam is a 96 oz pack. If they only eat Spam twice a month, how long before that pack goes bad? Is 96 oz really a "best buy" for her?
  2. Costco requires a $65/year membership. If Auntie Malia would save $8/month by shopping there, is it worth it? Show your math.
  3. Times Kalihi is 4 minutes from her house. Costco Iwilei is 25 minutes plus parking. Gas costs about $0.60/mile and each Costco round-trip is 12 miles. What does the "convenience discount" of Times need to be, per shopping trip, to break even?
  4. Auntie Malia has $80 today. The big Costco rice (25 lb) alone is $34.99. If she buys the biggest of every item — Spam $22.99, rice $34.99, shoyu $9.99 — she spends $67.97 and has $12 left for everything else. Is the "cheapest per ounce" the same as the "cheapest today"? Explain.

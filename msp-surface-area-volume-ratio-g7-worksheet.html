Why small things (like cells) have big SA:V.

1️⃣ What Is SA:V?

The RATIO of surface area to volume shows how much surface a shape has per unit of volume. SMALLER objects have BIGGER SA:V.
Formula for a cube with side s: SA = 6s², V = s³, so SA:V = 6/s.

Practice:

  1. Cube side 1 cm. SA = ? V = ? SA:V?
  2. Cube side 2 cm. SA = ? V = ? SA:V?
  3. Cube side 4 cm. SA = ? V = ? SA:V?

Answer Key — Section 1

(1) SA=6, V=1, ratio=6:1 (2) SA=24, V=8, ratio=3:1 (3) SA=96, V=64, ratio=1.5:1

3️⃣ Why This Matters (Cells)

Cells need to EXCHANGE nutrients through their surface. Higher SA:V = more efficient exchange. That's why cells are TINY.

Practice:

  1. Small cell: side 1 μm. SA:V?
  2. Big imaginary cell: side 5 μm. SA:V?
  3. Why can't cells be as big as basketballs?

Answer Key — Section 3

(1) 6:1 (2) 1.2:1 (3) SA:V would be tiny, so cell couldn't get nutrients in fast enough.

4️⃣ Real Applications

Broken-up ice melts faster, small mice lose more heat per gram, radiator fins have huge SA:V.

Practice:

  1. Why does crushed ice melt faster than one big cube?
  2. Why do animals in cold climates tend to be big (whales, bears)?
  3. Why are lungs and intestines wrinkly?

Answer Key — Section 4

(1) More surface area exposed to warm air/water (2) Big animals have lower SA:V — lose less body heat per volume (3) To increase surface area for gas exchange or absorption

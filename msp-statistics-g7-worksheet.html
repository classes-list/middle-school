1️⃣ Random Samples

A RANDOM sample is chosen without bias. Larger samples generally give better estimates.

Practice:

  1. Best way to sample all 7th graders: ask friends OR use random ID numbers?
  2. If a sample is too small, is it reliable?
  3. Why not survey only students in one classroom?

Answer Key — Section 1

(1) Random IDs (2) No — high variability (3) Not representative — bias

2️⃣ Mean and Range

Same as G6. Mean = sum ÷ count. Range = max − min.

Practice:

  1. Data 3,5,7,9,11. Mean?
  2. Same. Range?
  3. Data 20,25,30,35,40. Mean?
  4. Data 5,5,5,5,5. Range?

Answer Key — Section 2

(1) 7 (2) 8 (3) 30 (4) 0

3️⃣ Comparing Two Populations

Compare MEANS and RANGES.

Practice:

  1. Group A mean 80, range 20; Group B mean 85, range 10. Who is HIGHER on average? Who is MORE CONSISTENT?
  2. Sample A: 70, 80, 90. Sample B: 75, 80, 85. Means? Which more consistent (smaller range)?

Answer Key — Section 3

(1) B higher on avg; B more consistent (2) both mean 80; B more consistent (range 10 vs 20)

4️⃣ Inferences from Samples

A sample tells us about the whole population — but sample size and randomness matter.

Practice:

  1. A random sample of 100 students shows 60 like pizza. Estimate for 500 students?
  2. Class of 30. Sample of 5 students say they play soccer. How many in class (est)?
  3. Why is a sample of 10 less reliable than 100?

Answer Key — Section 4

(1) 300 like pizza (60%) (2) 30 (all — need more info) or you'd estimate proportionally (3) Less data means more chance of extreme values by chance

📐 Formulas (always available for reference):
Error = |Measured − Actual|
Percent Error = |Measured − Actual| ÷ Actual × 100
Percent error < absolute error can mislead — a big number of grams may still be a very small % of the real value.

Answer Key

Fill-in-the-blanks (under the target diagram):
Precision is how close your data is when you repeat the experiment.
Accuracy is how close your data is to the expected value.

(1) 3,600 m · (2) 4.25 g · (3) 500 mL · (4) 2.5 m · (5) 2,400 g · (6) 8,000 mg · (7) Error = |Measured − Actual|. %Error = |Measured − Actual| ÷ Actual × 100 · (8) Error = |45−50| = 5 g. %Error = 5/50 × 100 = 10% · (9) Error = 1 kg. %Error = 1/8 × 100 = 12.5% · (10) Error = 5,000 kg. %Error = 5000/180000 × 100 = 2.78% · (11) The whale (2.78% error) is more accurate than the cat (12.5% error). Absolute error alone is misleading — 5,000 kg sounds huge, but relative to a 180,000 kg whale it's less than 3% of the real value. Percent error compares the mistake to the size of the thing being measured, so it fairly compares accuracy across different scales. · (12) Paper: 10%. Elephant: 1.67%. Elephant is more accurate. · (13) Pencil: 7.5%. Road: 1%. Road is more accurate (even though the road was off by 100 meters vs the pencil's 1.5 cm). · (14) Top-left target (shots grouped tightly at the bullseye). · (15) Top-right target — shots grouped tightly but not near the bullseye. This is systematic error (a bias — like a scale that always reads 2 g too high). · (16) Bottom-left target — shots scattered but centered around the bullseye. This is reproducibility (random) error. · (17) Accuracy = how close your measurement is to the true value. Precision = how close repeated measurements are to each other. You can be one, both, or neither. · (18) Bar graph (or column chart) · (19) Scatter plot (X-Y plot) · (20) Mean = (12+18+25+30+22)/5 = 107/5 = 21.4; Range = 30 − 12 = 18

📘 How It Works

Key idea: Horizontal: v cos θ · t. Vertical: h + v sin θ · t − 16t².

Split the launch speed into parts: horizontal v cos θ (never changes) and vertical v sin θ (gravity pulls it down: −16t² in feet). Set y = 0 and use the quadratic formula to find the landing time; plug that time into x for the range. The top of the path is at t = v sin θ / 32.

👀 Watch Two Examples

✏️ Worked Example 1
A ball is launched at 96 ft/s at 30° from a height of 6 ft: y = 6 + (96 sin 30°)t − 16t². When does it land? (seconds, nearest hundredth)
Answer: 3.12
✏️ Worked Example 2
A ball is launched at 64 ft/s at 45° from a height of 6 ft. What is its maximum height? (feet, nearest tenth)
Answer: 38.0

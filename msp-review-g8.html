Section 1. Metric Conversion

The Rules

  • Metric units are based on powers of 10 — each step on the metric staircase = ×10.
  • Base units: meter — length (m). Liter — volume (L, capital). Gram — mass (g). Prefixes work the same across all three.
  • Moving DOWN the staircase (to a SMALLER unit) → decimal moves to the RIGHT (multiply by 10 per step).
  • Moving UP the staircase (to a LARGER unit) → decimal moves to the LEFT (divide by 10 per step).
  • Count the STEPS between the two units — that is how many places the decimal moves.
  • Add trailing zeros when needed (3.5 → 3500) or leading zeros (250 → 0.250) so the shift works.

The Metric Staircase (each step down: multiply by 10 · each step up: divide by 10)

kilo (k)hecto (h)deka (da)UNIT (m,L,g)deci (d)centi (c)milli (m)

Examples

Example 1 Convert 3.5 km to meters. km → m is 3 steps DOWN. Move the decimal 3 places RIGHT: 3.5 → 3500. Answer: 3,500 m.
Example 2 Convert 250 mg to grams. mg → g is 3 steps UP. Move the decimal 3 places LEFT: 250. → 0.250. Answer: 0.25 g.

Section 2. Experimental Error — Accuracy & Precision

The Rules

  • ACCURACY = how close your measurement is to the ACTUAL (accepted) value.
  • PRECISION = how close REPEATED measurements are to EACH OTHER.
  • Error = the absolute value of ( Measured Value − Actual Value ). Always positive.
  • Percent Error = ( |Measured Value − Actual Value| ÷ Actual Value ) × 100%.
  • Smaller percent error → more accurate. 0% error means measured value = actual value exactly.
  • Average of trials = (sum of all trials) ÷ (number of trials).
  • More trials averaged together usually reduces random error — that is why scientists repeat measurements.

Accuracy vs Precision at a glance

AccuracyPrecision
What it measuresHow close to the ACTUAL valueHow close REPEATED measurements are to each other
Dartboard analogyDarts land near the bullseyeDarts land clustered together (anywhere on the board)

FormulaPercent Error = ( |Measured − Actual| ÷ Actual ) × 100%(none — judged by how tightly repeated trials cluster)

Examples

Example 1 A student measures a book's mass as 195 g. The actual mass is 200 g. Error = |195 − 200| = 5 g. Percent Error = ( 5 ÷ 200 ) × 100 = 2.5%.
Example 2 Three trials give 12.1, 12.3, 12.2 mL. Average = (12.1 + 12.3 + 12.2) ÷ 3 = 36.6 ÷ 3 = 12.2 mL. The trials are precise (close together); accuracy depends on the actual value.

Section 3. Integer Operations & Sign Rules

The ONE Sign Rule (works for × ÷ AND every stacked sign)

  • SAME signs → POSITIVE. ( + · + ) = + and ( − · − ) = +
  • DIFFERENT signs → NEGATIVE. ( + · − ) = − and ( − · + ) = −
  • This one rule works for multiplication, division, AND every time two signs sit next to each other (like −(−9) or 5 + (−2)).

The Trick — "Hidden Multiplication"

  • Every sign in front of a number is really "multiplied by ±1." −a really means (−1)(a). +a really means (+1)(a).
  • Two stacked signs like −(−9) are really (−1)(−1)(9) = (+1)(9) = +9. Apply the sign rule to the two signs first, THEN multiply the number.
  • Same idea for +(−2) = (+1)(−2) = −2. And −(+2) = (−1)(+2) = −2. And +(+2) = (+1)(+2) = +2.
  • Once you collapse the stacked signs, you are left with a single sign and a number — a much easier problem.

Subtraction is really addition in disguise

  • GOLDEN RULE: a − b = a + (−b). ALWAYS rewrite subtraction as "adding the opposite."
  • After you rewrite, use the sign-collapse trick to clean up any stacked signs, then add on the number line.
  • Number-line story for addition: positive → move RIGHT. negative → move LEFT. Start at the first number and move by the second.

Sign-Collapse Cheat Sheet

Examples — collapse signs first, then finish

Example 1 — multiply, different signs (−3) × 4. Sign rule: − and + → answer is NEGATIVE. 3 × 4 = 12. Answer: −12.
Example 2 — multiply, same signs (−5) × (−2). Sign rule: − and − → POSITIVE. 5 × 2 = 10. Answer: +10.
Example 3 — divide, different signs −20 ÷ 4. Sign rule: − and + → NEGATIVE. 20 ÷ 4 = 5. Answer: −5.
Example 4 — standalone double negative −(−9). The two stacked signs (− and −) collapse to a + by the sign rule. So −(−9) = +9. Answer: +9.
Example 5 — plus a negative 5 + (−2). The stacked signs (+ and −) collapse to a single −. So 5 + (−2) becomes 5 − 2 = 3. Answer: 3.
Example 6 — minus a negative 5 − (−2). The stacked signs (− and −) collapse to a +. So 5 − (−2) becomes 5 + 2 = 7. Answer: 7.
Example 7 — negative minus positive −7 − 2. Rewrite as −7 + (−2). Nothing to collapse (already a single − in front of 2). Number-line: start at −7, move 2 more LEFT. Answer: −9.
Example 8 — negative plus negative −7 + (−3). Collapse (+ and −) to a single −. So −7 + (−3) becomes −7 − 3. Number-line: start at −7, move 3 more LEFT. Answer: −10.
Example 9 — same-sign addition (−6) + (−8). Collapse (+ and −) → −. So (−6) + (−8) = −6 − 8. Both signs pull LEFT on the number line: 6 + 8 = 14, and it is negative. Answer: −14.

Section 4. Scientific Notation

The Rules

  • Scientific notation form: a × 10ⁿ where 1 ≤ |a| < 10 and n is an integer.
  • LARGE numbers → decimal moves LEFT → POSITIVE exponent. SMALL numbers → decimal moves RIGHT → NEGATIVE exponent.
  • MULTIPLY: (a × 10ᵐ)(b × 10ⁿ) = (a·b) × 10^(m+n). Multiply coefficients, ADD exponents.
  • DIVIDE: (a × 10ᵐ) ÷ (b × 10ⁿ) = (a÷b) × 10^(m−n). Divide coefficients, SUBTRACT exponents.
  • ADD / SUBTRACT: MATCH the exponents first, then add or subtract the coefficients, then adjust.
  • ADJUST if the coefficient is not between 1 and 10: ≥ 10 → shift decimal LEFT, INCREASE exponent; < 1 → shift RIGHT, DECREASE exponent.

Examples

Example 1 — simple conversion Write 4,500,000 in scientific notation. Move decimal 6 places LEFT. Answer: 4.5 × 10⁶.
Example 2 — must adjust Simplify 10 × 10⁴. Rewrite 10 = 1.0 × 10¹. Combine: 10 × 10⁴ = 1.0 × 10¹ × 10⁴ = 1.0 × 10⁵. Answer: 1.0 × 10⁵.
Example 3 — multiply Multiply (3 × 10⁴)(2 × 10³). Multiply the coefficients: 3 × 2 = 6. Add the exponents: 4 + 3 = 7. Answer: 6 × 10⁷.
Example 4 — multiply (must adjust) Multiply (5 × 10³)(4 × 10²). Coefficients: 5 × 4 = 20. Exponents: 3 + 2 = 5. Result 20 × 10⁵ needs adjusting: 20 = 2.0 × 10¹, so 20 × 10⁵ = 2.0 × 10⁶. Answer: 2.0 × 10⁶.
Example 5 — divide Divide (8 × 10⁶) ÷ (2 × 10²). Divide the coefficients: 8 ÷ 2 = 4. Subtract the exponents: 6 − 2 = 4. Answer: 4 × 10⁴.
Example 6 — divide (must adjust) Divide (3 × 10⁵) ÷ (6 × 10²). Coefficients: 3 ÷ 6 = 0.5. Exponents: 5 − 2 = 3. Result 0.5 × 10³ needs adjusting: 0.5 = 5.0 × 10⁻¹, so 0.5 × 10³ = 5.0 × 10². Answer: 5.0 × 10².
Example 7 — add (match exponents) Add (3.2 × 10⁵) + (4.5 × 10⁴). Rewrite 4.5 × 10⁴ = 0.45 × 10⁵. Add coefficients: (3.2 + 0.45) × 10⁵ = 3.65 × 10⁵. Answer: 3.65 × 10⁵.
Example 8 — subtract (match exponents) Subtract (7.2 × 10⁶) − (5 × 10⁵). Rewrite 5 × 10⁵ = 0.5 × 10⁶. Subtract coefficients: (7.2 − 0.5) × 10⁶ = 6.7 × 10⁶. Answer: 6.7 × 10⁶.

Section 5. Real Numbers — Rational vs Irrational

The Rules

  • A RATIONAL number can be written as a fraction p/q of two integers (q ≠ 0). Its decimal EITHER terminates OR repeats.
  • An IRRATIONAL number CANNOT be written as such a fraction. Its decimal goes on forever without repeating.
  • Common irrationals: π, √2, √3, √5, √n where n is NOT a perfect square, e (Euler's number).
  • A REPEATING decimal (0.333…, 0.272727…) is rational — it can be rewritten as a fraction.
  • To convert a repeating decimal to a fraction: let x = the decimal, multiply both sides by 10ⁿ (n = length of the repeating block), subtract, then solve for x.
  • REAL NUMBERS = all rationals + all irrationals. Every point on the number line is a real number.

Examples

Example 1 (classifying) Classify: √16, √7, 3.25, π, 0.333…, 0.121121112… → √16 = 4 (rational). √7 (irrational). 3.25 (rational, terminates). π (irrational). 0.333… = 1/3 (rational). 0.121121112… (irrational).
Example 2 (repeating → fraction) Convert 0.4444… to a fraction. Let x = 0.4444…. Multiply by 10: 10x = 4.4444…. Subtract: 10x − x = 4.4444… − 0.4444… → 9x = 4 → x = 4/9.

Section 6. Exponents — Laws of Exponents

The Rules

  • PRODUCT: aᵐ · aⁿ = a^(m+n). Same base → ADD the exponents.
  • QUOTIENT: aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = a^(m−n). Same base → SUBTRACT the exponents.
  • POWER of a POWER: (aᵐ)ⁿ = a^(m·n). MULTIPLY the exponents.
  • POWER of a PRODUCT: (a·b)ⁿ = aⁿ · bⁿ. Distribute the exponent to each factor.
  • ZERO exponent: a⁰ = 1 for any nonzero base.
  • NEGATIVE exponent: a⁻ⁿ = 1 / aⁿ. A negative exponent means "flip to the denominator."
  • These rules ONLY apply when the BASES are the SAME. 2³ · 3² does NOT simplify by adding exponents.
  • Never multiply the base by the exponent. 2³ ≠ 6. 2³ = 2 · 2 · 2 = 8.

Example 2 — negative + zero Simplify: (3⁻²)(3⁵)(3⁰). Same base, add exponents: 3^(−2 + 5 + 0) = 3³ = 27.

🎯 GCF & LCM — See It, Then Solve It

Three word problems. Three interactive visuals. One decider.

The one-question decider: Before touching numbers, ask yourself —
"Am I splitting stuff APART into equal groups, or waiting for two events to LINE UP again?"
Splitting apart → GCF (biggest group size, answer is SMALLER).
Lining up → LCM (next meeting time, answer is BIGGER).

1️⃣ Emma's Gift Bags (GCF)

Clue words: identical, no leftovers, largest number → GCF
Emma has 15 stickers 🌟 and 20 pencils ✏️. She wants to make identical gift bags with no leftovers. What is the largest number of bags she can make?
Pick a number of bags to try!
GCF method — list the factors:
Factors of 15: 1, 3, 5, 15
Factors of 20: 1, 2, 4, 5, 10, 20
Biggest shared factor = GCF(15, 20) = 5 bags
Each bag: 3 stickers + 4 pencils. Nothing left over. ✓
Sanity check: A GCF answer is always less than or equal to the smaller number. 5 ≤ 15 ✓

2️⃣ The Two Lighthouses (LCM)

Clue words: every ___ seconds, blink together, next → LCM
One lighthouse blinks every 6 seconds 🟡. Another blinks every 8 seconds 🔵. If they blink together at time 0, when will they next blink together?
Lighthouse A
every 6 sec
Lighthouse B
every 8 sec
⏱️ 0 sec
Timeline (seconds) — watch where 🟡 and 🔵 land on the same tick
LCM method — list the multiples:
Multiples of 6: 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36
Multiples of 8: 8, 16, 24, 32, 40
First shared multiple = LCM(6, 8) = 24 seconds
Sanity check: An LCM answer is always greater than or equal to the larger number. 24 ≥ 8 ✓

3️⃣ The Two Buses (LCM)

Clue words: every ___ minutes, leave together, next → LCM
Bus A leaves the station every 12 minutes 🚌. Bus B every 15 minutes 🚍. Both just left at noon. When will they next leave together?
🏢 Station
Bus A — every 12 min
🚌
Bus B — every 15 min
🚍
🕐 12:00
Timeline (minutes past noon) — 🚌 and 🚍 meet at minute 60
LCM method — list the multiples:
Multiples of 12: 12, 24, 36, 48, 60, 72
Multiples of 15: 15, 30, 45, 60, 75
First shared multiple = LCM(12, 15) = 60 minutes = 1:00 PM
Sanity check: 60 ≥ 15 ✓     And 12 × 15 = 180 is a common multiple, but not the LEAST — students who multiply usually miss the earlier meet at 60.

🌟 The two sentences worth memorizing:
"Sharing things → GCF → answer is SMALLER."
"Waiting for things → LCM → answer is BIGGER."

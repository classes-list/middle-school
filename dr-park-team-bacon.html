🌿 Why Feral Pig Eradication?

Feral pigs are an invasive species in Hawai‘i. Introduced to the islands centuries ago and now unmanaged in the wild, their population has exploded, causing serious environmental, agricultural, and public-health problems.

  • Destroying native watersheds — pigs root up soil in upland forests, accelerating erosion that clogs streams, damages coral reefs downstream, and reduces the aquifer’s ability to recharge Hawai‘i’s fresh-water supply.
  • Wiping out native plants & endangered species — they eat and trample rare native ferns, tree seedlings, and ground-nesting birds. Hawai‘i has some of the highest concentrations of endangered species in the world; feral pigs are a leading driver of that loss.
  • Spreading invasive plants — pigs carry the seeds of strawberry guava, banana poka, and other invasive plants deep into intact forests, giving those weeds a foothold.
  • Breeding mosquitoes that kill native birds — pig wallows fill with water and become mosquito nurseries. Those mosquitoes carry avian malaria, which has driven native forest birds like the ‘i‘iwi and ‘apapane to near-extinction.
  • Damaging farms & yards — pigs raid crops, tear up landscaping, and damage fences. They also carry diseases like leptospirosis and swine brucellosis that can transmit to livestock, pets, and people.
  • Traffic and safety hazards — growing populations in suburban areas lead to vehicle collisions and encounters with residents and pets.

Team Bacon partners with landowners and conservation groups across O‘ahu to remove feral pigs on foot — the traditional way — helping protect native ecosystems, water quality, and cultivated land.

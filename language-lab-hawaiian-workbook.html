🌏 Culture Corner

  • Hawaiian (ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi) is one of two official languages of the State of Hawaiʻi.
  • The Hawaiian alphabet has only 13 letters: A, E, I, O, U, H, K, L, M, N, P, W, and the ʻokina (glottal stop).
  • The kahakō (macron) over a vowel means to hold the sound longer -- listen for the difference between makai and mākai.
  • 'Aloha' is more than 'hello' -- it means love, breath of life, and mutual regard.
  • Immersion schools (Papahana Kaiapuni) have brought Hawaiian back from the edge of extinction since the 1980s.

📐 Mini-Grammar: ka vs. ke articles

Hawaiian has two words for 'the': ka and ke. Use ke before words starting with K, E, A, O (KEAO trick): ke kula, ke keiki, ke ʻano. Use ka before everything else: ka hale, ka puke, ka wai.

✏️ Try It Yourself
  1. Greet a partner with the right aloha for the time of day.
  2. Count from ʻekahi to iwakālua aloud.
  3. Name three things in the classroom using ka / ke correctly.
  4. Say your favorite color and one thing that is that color.

📐 Mini-Grammar: how to say 'I go' vs. 'you go'

Hawaiian verbs don't change form for person. Just use the verb + the pronoun. Hele au = I go. Hele ʻoe = You go. Hele ʻo ia = He/she goes. Very simple, but word order matters -- verb usually comes first.

📐 Mini-Grammar: past tense with 'Ua'

To make a verb past tense in Hawaiian, put ua in front of it: hele = go → ua hele = went. ʻai = eat → ua ʻai = ate. So 'I went' is Ua hele au.

🍪 Build a Recipe Scaler in Google Sheets

Turn a 24-cookie recipe into a 6-cookie or 60-cookie recipe with the tap of a key.

🎯 The Assignment

Your grandma's chocolate chip cookie recipe makes 24 cookies. But sometimes you want to bake for just yourself (say, 6 cookies), and sometimes you want to bake for a whole party (60 cookies). Instead of doing the math by hand every time, you're going to build a calculator that scales the recipe automatically.

Your finished spreadsheet will:

  • ✅ Show the original recipe for 24 cookies (fixed)
  • ✅ Have one input cell: how many cookies do you WANT?
  • ✅ Use a scale factor k = new ÷ old that updates automatically
  • ✅ Show every ingredient scaled to the new amount
  • ✅ Round eggs UP (you can't use 2.5 eggs!)
  • ✅ Have "preset" buttons for common batch sizes (12, 36, 48)
💡 Why this works: Every ingredient in the scaled recipe = original amount × k. That's the definition of a proportional relationship — y = kx.

1 Set Up Your Sheet

  1. Go to sheets.google.com and sign in with your school Gmail.
  2. Click + Blank spreadsheet.
  3. Rename it: Recipe Scaler — [Your Name]
  4. In cell A1, type a big title: 🍪 Grandma's Chocolate Chip Cookies. Format bold, font size 18.

2 Enter the Original Recipe

3 Add the "How Many Cookies?" Input

This is the only cell the user changes. Everything else recomputes from it.

The scale factor:   =B15/24
🧠 What it means: new number of cookies ÷ original 24 cookies
For 36 cookies: k = 36 ÷ 24 = 1.5   (1.5× everything!)
For 6 cookies: k = 6 ÷ 24 = 0.25   (quarter of everything)
For 48 cookies: k = 48 ÷ 24 = 2   (double batch)
💡 Format B15 yellow so users know that's where they type. Select B15 → Fill color → light yellow. Make B16 bold.

The proportional formula:   =B4*$B$16
🧠 What it means: original amount × k = new amount
📌 The $ signs on $B$16 lock it, so when you drag down, it always points at the scale factor.
📌 This IS y = kx — where x is the original amount, k is the scale factor, and y is the new amount!

Speed trick: fill down

  1. Type =B4*$B$16 in cell D4. Press Enter.
  2. Click D4 again, grab the little blue square in the bottom-right corner, drag down through D12.
  3. Every ingredient auto-fills with its scaled amount.
💡 Change B15 to 60. Watch every ingredient in column D update INSTANTLY. That's the power of proportional formulas.

5 Handle the Egg Problem

Eggs are a whole number — you can't use 2.5 eggs! Update the egg row (D11) with a rounding formula:

D11:   =CEILING(B11*$B$16, 1)

What CEILING does: rounds UP to the nearest whole number. So 2.5 eggs → 3 eggs. 4.2 eggs → 5 eggs.

⚠️ Why round UP? When baking, having a little extra egg is fine, but too little is disaster. You'd rather waste half an egg than have a dry cookie. Real chefs round up.

Optional: Also round chocolate chips

Chocolate chips come in bags. You might want to round chips to the nearest 0.25 cup (a quarter cup):

D12:   =CEILING(B12*$B$16, 0.25)

7 Final Formatting

  • 🎨 Color-code sections: input cells (B15) yellow, scale factor (B16) light green, scaled amounts (D4:D12) light blue.
  • 📏 Widen columns: right-click column headers → Resize column to fit.
  • 🔢 Format Column D as numbers with 2 decimal places (except D11 which is a whole number).
  • 🔒 Bold the important cells: B15 (input), B16 (scale factor), and all of Column D.
  • 🖼️ Add a border around your input area (A15:B16) to make it stand out.

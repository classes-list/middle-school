⚛️ Radioactive Decay & Half-Life

Every half-life, half the atoms decay. Watch the pattern.

Big idea: A half-life is the time it takes for exactly half of the remaining radioactive atoms to decay. After 1 half-life, half remain. After 2, a quarter. After 3, an eighth. This halving pattern is exponential decay — same math geologists use to date rocks billions of years old.

Notice: the atoms never quite reach zero — each step just halves what's still there. In real physics, after enough half-lives it becomes undetectable, but the math itself keeps halving forever.

🧭 Why This Is Fractions in Action

Radioactive decay is multiplying by ½ over and over:

  • After 1 half-life: 1 × ½ = ½ (half remaining)
  • After 2 half-lives: ½ × ½ = ¼ (quarter remaining)
  • After 3 half-lives: ¼ × ½ = ⅛ (eighth remaining)
  • After n half-lives: (½)n remaining

Powers of ½ are the same idea as powers of 10 in the Richter scale — just going the other direction. Powers of 10 grow; powers of ½ shrink. Both are exponential patterns, and both are why big numbers or tiny fractions describe things the real world does effortlessly.

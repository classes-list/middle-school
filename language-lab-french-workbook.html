🌏 Culture Corner

  • French is spoken by ~300 million people across 29 countries, including Canada, Switzerland, Belgium, and much of West Africa.
  • France is the most-visited country in the world.
  • French has masculine (le) and feminine (la) nouns -- and adjectives change to match.
  • Silent letters everywhere! 'Paris' is pronounced 'Pah-REE' -- the S is silent.
  • About 30 percent of English words come from French (via the Norman Conquest of 1066).

📐 Mini-Grammar: gender & articles

Every French noun is masculine (le) or feminine (la). Words starting with a vowel use l' (l'école, l'eau). Adjectives change to match: un chat noir (a black cat, masc.) but une maison blanche (a white house, fem.).

  2. Count from 1 to 30 aloud without stopping.
  3. Name three classroom objects with the correct le/la/l'.
  4. Say the day, month, and weather in one French sentence.

📐 Mini-Grammar: present-tense -ER verbs

Regular -ER verbs like parler (to speak) drop -er and add: je parle, tu parles, il/elle parle, nous parlons, vous parlez, ils/elles parlent. Same rule for étudier, écouter, regarder, travailler, chanter.

📐 Mini-Grammar: passé composé

To talk about a completed past action, use passé composé: avoir/être + past participle. For -ER verbs, the past participle ends in -é: parler → parlé; manger → mangé. Example: J'ai parlé = I spoke; Nous avons mangé = we ate.

✏️ Try It Yourself

  2. Describe how you felt on your first day of middle school (use 'parce que').
  3. Plan an imaginary trip to Paris: 3 places + 3 things to buy.
  4. Give directions from the classroom to the library in French.

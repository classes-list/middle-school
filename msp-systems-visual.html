📐 Systems of Equations — Where Do They Meet?

Two equations. Two lines. One intersection = one solution.

The big idea: A system of two linear equations is just two lines on the same graph. The solution is the point (x, y) where they intersect — because that's the ONLY point on BOTH lines at the same time.

🎯 Try It: Move The Sliders, Watch The Lines

Line A:
y = 1x + 2
Line B:
y = -1x + 4
Solution: (1, 3)

🧠 Three Cases

Case 1 — Different slopes (m₁ ≠ m₂): Lines cross exactly once. One unique solution (x, y).
Case 2 — Same slope, different y-intercepts: Parallel lines. They never cross. No solution.
Case 3 — Same slope AND same y-intercept: The SAME line drawn twice. Every point is a solution. Infinite solutions.

🛒 Ratios & Unit Rates — See The Pattern

Set a ratio. Buy in batches. Watch how the numbers scale together.

The big idea: A ratio like 3 : 2 means for every 3 apples, there are 2 oranges. When you scale it up (buy more batches), both numbers grow — but the ratio stays the same. A unit rate asks: how many of one thing per ONE of the other?

The pattern to notice: Every row of the table has the SAME reduced ratio. That's what makes it a proportional relationship. If you divide apples ÷ oranges in ANY row, you get the same number — the constant of proportionality (k).

🧠 Two Ways to Think About It

Ratio thinking: "For every 3 apples, there are 2 oranges." Groups items together.
Unit rate thinking: "There are 1.5 apples per 1 orange." Answers "per one" questions — perfect for comparing prices, speeds, best deals.
Best-deal shortcut: When comparing two offers, ALWAYS convert to unit rate ($/oz, mi/hr, apples/orange). Smaller price per unit = better deal.

🌏 Culture Corner

  • Spanish is spoken by ~500 million people worldwide -- the second-most-spoken first language after Mandarin.
  • In Hawai'i, many local families have Puerto Rican and Filipino roots that share Spanish vocabulary through history.
  • Fun fact: Spanish uses upside-down ¿ and ¡ to open questions and exclamations.
  • Every Spanish noun is masculine (el) or feminine (la) -- and adjectives change to match.
  • Rolling the R in perro (dog) versus tapping the R in pero (but) can change the meaning of a word!

📐 Mini-Grammar: gender & articles

Every Spanish noun is masculine or feminine. Use el for masculine (el libro), la for feminine (la casa). Most nouns ending in -o are masculine and most ending in -a are feminine. Adjectives change to match: un gato negro (a black cat, masc.) but una casa blanca (a white house, fem.).

✏️ Try It Yourself
  1. Introduce yourself to a partner: name, age, where you're from.
  2. Count from 1 to 30 aloud without stopping.
  3. Name three classroom objects with the correct el/la.
  4. Say the day, month, and weather in one Spanish sentence.

📐 Mini-Grammar: present-tense -AR verbs

Regular -AR verbs like hablar (to speak) drop -ar and add these endings: yo hablo, tú hablas, él/ella habla, nosotros hablamos, ellos/ustedes hablan. Same rule applies to estudiar, escuchar, mirar, trabajar, cantar.

✏️ Try It Yourself
  1. Describe your morning routine using at least 5 verbs.
  2. Describe your best friend in 3 Spanish sentences.
  3. Order a full Spanish lunch: 3 foods + 1 drink.
  4. Tell a partner what time you wake up and what time you go to bed.

📐 Mini-Grammar: preterite endings

To talk about a completed past action, use the preterite. For -AR verbs, drop -ar and add: -é, -aste, -ó, -amos, -aron. Example: hablar → yo hablé (I spoke), ella habló (she spoke).

✏️ Try It Yourself
  1. Write about a real trip you took, using at least 4 preterite verbs.
  2. Describe how you felt on your first day of middle school, and why (use 'porque').
  3. Plan an imaginary trip to a Spanish-speaking country: 3 places + 3 things you'll buy.
  4. Give directions from the classroom to the library in Spanish.
